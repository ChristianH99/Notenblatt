Step 1 · Prepare

A class is set up in two minutes

Copy the name list from Excel or ASV and paste it. Notenblatt recognises last and first names, even in two columns. For a new exam, copy the tasks and maximum points of an earlier one, for a parallel class or a make-up exam.

  • Classes belong to a school year; for the next year, copy a class with all its students and subjects.
  • Each subject has its own weighting, grading system and rounding. Defaults for new subjects are set once.

Step 2 · Mark

Enter one task for the whole class without touching the mouse

Type the points and press Enter: the cursor moves to the next student. You mark task by task, just like working through the stack of papers. Every entry is saved immediately; there is no Save button.

Step 3 · Evaluate

Grade key, distribution and average while you mark

The evaluation next to the table updates with every entry: point limits per grade, count and share, average, share of grades 5 and 6. Notenblatt calculates with whole numbers instead of decimals, so there are no rounding errors as in Excel.

Error grading for dictations

In subjects graded 1–6 an exam can get an extra “Fehler” (errors) column. The grade combines the grade for points and the grade for errors in the ratio you set.

Example: dictation 1, class 5a

Grade for points (12.5 of 20)2.8
3 errors × 0.3, from 1.01.9
Points : errors = 1 : 1, truncated2.3

Step 4 · Final mark

The report card grade per subject, weighted and traceable

A subject's grade overview shows all marks of the school year, the average per category, the weighted overall average and the final mark of every student. Oral marks are entered directly, without points.

Weighting of 9b English

Schulaufgaben ×2Small tests ×1Oral ×1

50 % · 25 % · 25 %. You set weights and categories per subject.

Your rules, not a spreadsheet template's

Round half up, at exactly x.5 in the student's favour, or truncate. Final mark as a whole grade or with one decimal. Rounding always starts from the exact value, never from already rounded intermediate results.

Grades of different subjects are never averaged together: each subject of a class has its own overview and its own weighting.

Step 5 · Safeguard

Your grades stay with you, encrypted if you want

The database lives in your user profile or in a folder you choose, for example on the school's network drive. That way you use the same data in the staff room and at home, on one device at a time.

  • AES-256 for the database, password at start. Without the password the data is lost; there is no back door.
  • Backup as a single file, always encrypted with your password, e.g. for a USB stick or moving to a new device.
  • Network drive briefly gone? Your entries stay in the app and are saved as soon as the folder is reachable again.
  • Updates in the app: Notenblatt shows what is new and only installs when you agree. Your data is kept.

Privacy and technology

No server, no account: student data never leaves your device

Data protection (GDPR)
No telemetry, no error reports, no ads. Deleted students, classes and exams are also physically removed from the file. Notenblatt points out classes whose school year ended more than 12 months ago (configurable: 6, 12 or 24 months, or off).
Storage, not hosting
There is no Notenblatt server and no cloud. The data lives in %APPDATA%\Notenblatt or in a folder you choose, such as your school's network drive.
Roles and permissions
Notenblatt is a tool for one teacher, without user accounts or roles. Access is protected by your Windows account and the encryption password. A database is open on one device at a time.
Offline
Everything works without the internet. The only connection is the update check against GitHub, which sends no data from Notenblatt. You can turn it off in the settings.
Devices
Windows 10 and 11 on PCs and laptops (x64) and ARM devices such as Surface Pro X and Surface Pro 11, with a touch mode. There is no version for macOS, iPad or Linux.
Open source
Notenblatt is released under the MIT licence. The source code is on GitHub, open to your school's IT or data protection officer.

Whether you may process student data on a private device is decided by your federal state or school, often with approval from the head teacher. Notenblatt makes the technical requirements easier (encryption, deletion periods) but does not replace that approval.

Questions

What colleagues ask before switching

What does Notenblatt cost?

Nothing. Notenblatt is free and open source (MIT licence), with no ads, no subscription and no account.

How long does it take to learn?

On first start, a five-step tour walks you through the main settings; it takes about a minute. Then you create a class, paste the names and enter your first exam. If you know the Excel grading sheet, you will feel at home: the table looks similar.

Can I get my data out again if I stop using it?

Yes. Every exam and every grade overview exports to CSV for Excel, and to PDF. The backup (.notenblatt) contains all data, encrypted with your password, and can be restored on any device with Notenblatt.

Does the school's IT have to install or approve anything?

No. The installer needs no administrator rights and installs for your user account only. There is no server to run and no accounts to manage. Updates come through the app itself.

Because the installer is not signed, Windows shows a SmartScreen warning on first launch; here is what to do.

Can I bring over marks from my old Excel sheet?

Yes, by copy and paste: names via “Namen einfügen…”, points as a selected range with Ctrl+V straight into the table. If a value is invalid, for example above the maximum, nothing is pasted and Notenblatt tells you which one.

What if I forget the password?

Then the encrypted data is lost. There is deliberately no back door, not even for the developer. Keep the password safe, for example in a password manager.

Can I work on two devices?

Yes, one after the other. Put the database in a shared folder, such as a network drive, and choose it on both devices. If it is open on one device, the other one tells you. Keep Notenblatt at the same version on both.

Is the app available in English?

No. Notenblatt is made for German schools and its interface is German. This page and the release notes are also available in English.
